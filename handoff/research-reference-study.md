## PART 1: kodryx.ai (home + /about-us/)

Measured facts:
- **Type:** Space Grotesk throughout. H1 80px/90px weight 700, letter-spacing -1px, white. H2 42px/50px weight 700, navy rgb(10,22,94). H3 26px/34px. Body 17px/27px, grey rgb(88,91,111). The CTA band heading is 52px.
- **Colour:** accent blue rgb(43,77,255); light grey band rgb(245,246,247); page is about 16,640px tall.
- **Build:** a WordPress/Elementor theme ("techtlk") with a lot of leftover template content.

Execution principles:

1. **One-idea hero on a full-bleed dark image.** Eyebrow, a 5-6 word H1, a one-line subhead, a single CTA, then a trust badge and partner-logo strip inside the same block (about 1,220px tall). Why it works: promise, action and credibility land before any scrolling.
2. **Steep, simple type scale in one family.** 80 / 42 / 26 / 17px with tight tracking on headings only. Why: hierarchy comes from size contrast, so the page reads as designed without extra fonts.
3. **Two-tone text: navy headings, mid-grey body.** Why: headings carry the scan path and long copy recedes without hurting legibility.
4. **Thin saturated ticker as a seam.** An 89px accent-blue marquee of service labels sits directly under the hero. Why: it marks the dark-to-light transition and conveys breadth in almost no vertical space.
5. **Eyebrow + H2 on every section.** A small category label sits above a plain-language heading ("Our Core Services" above the H2). Why: scanners get a map from the eyebrows, readers get the claim from the H2.
6. **Sparing dark/light rhythm.** Dark hero, white, light-grey services, one full navy band (about 950px) for value drivers, white again, grey close. Why: one dark interruption mid-page resets attention; dark is reserved for the hero and the single most important argument.
7. **Alternating density.** Short strips (89px ticker, 262px partner carousel, 111px CTA band) sit between tall sections of 760-950px. Why: the short bands act as breathing points and stop a card-wall feel.
8. **Proof in layers, not one block.** Rating badge in the hero, an animated year counter, a partner carousel, one large "25+ Years" stat beside the why-us cards, then named testimonials with role and company. Why: credibility is re-supplied at each point of doubt.
9. **Testimonials carry a concrete number.** One quote cites "30+ property agreements". Why: a specific figure in a customer's voice is more believable than a company-stated statistic.
10. **Services as a small set of equal cards (five) with outcome titles; tech stack as a separate logo-only wall (about 20).** Why: separating "what we do" from "what we use" keeps services readable and lets logos work as fast recognition.
11. **Case studies get the most room.** The portfolio section is about 6,700px, roughly 40% of the page, as large stacked project blocks rather than a thumbnail grid. Why: built work is the strongest argument, so it gets the most scroll.
12. **Industries as a compact tile grid with a framing line** (agnostic, but strongest in eight named sectors). Why: visitors self-identify quickly without a page per sector.
13. **CTA repeats in three registers.** Hero button, a slim mid-page band with a large heading plus phone number, and a closing section with a question heading and contact details. The phone number also sits inline in the about block. Why: every scroll depth has an exit to contact.
14. **Restrained motion and slim navigation.** Fade-in-up/left/right entrances (eight animated elements in the hero), one marquee, one counter, one logo carousel; no scroll-jacking. The header is transparent over the hero, switches to a fixed sticky bar on scroll, and has six top-level items with one two-group dropdown. Why: motion signals polish without costing reading time, and the nav stays out of the hero's way.

Process: no process or "how we work" section exists on the home or about page, so there is nothing to learn from on that point.

Do not copy:
- **Wording:** "AI That Builds Competitive Advantage", "Practical AI. Measurable Impact.", "UNLEASH AI", "Built by the best of builders", "What would you get?", section headings, service and industry names as phrased, testimonial text.
- **Brand marks and names:** the Kodryx logo; product names LegalLens, VartaIQ, ReduCO2e, DocuCA, Cyber Saathi; the Trustpilot badge; partner and tech logos.
- **Visual identity:** Space Grotesk combined with the navy rgb(10,22,94) and blue rgb(43,77,255) palette; the hero banner image.
- **Exact layout:** the section order and the theme's card and shape decorations.
- **Their mistakes:** placeholder contacts (support@company.com, a Fall River MA address), insurance copy on the about page, typos ("AI Eductaion", "What Saying Our Customers"), a counter that reads "00 + Yrs", a CTA linking to the theme vendor's demo domain, and dead "#" social links.
